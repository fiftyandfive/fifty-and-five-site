Cost and coverage

How much does private pay speech therapy cost in Orlando?

At The Visiting Voice, a 60 minute private pay session is $175, a 45 minute session is $135, and a 30 minute session is $100. A comprehensive evaluation with a written report is $275. Each private pay home visit adds a flat $25 travel fee.

Does Medicare cover speech therapy at home in Florida?

Yes. Medicare Part B covers outpatient speech therapy when it is medically necessary, including visits at home or in assisted living, and the patient does not need to be homebound. A physician, nurse practitioner, or physician assistant must certify the plan of care, and the usual Part B deductible and coinsurance apply. The practice's Medicare enrollment is in progress, so Medicare is not billed yet.

Do you take private insurance?

The practice is private pay for commercial insurance, and we provide a superbill with the codes your insurer needs so you can request reimbursement directly. Whether your plan pays anything back depends on your plan, and reimbursement is not guaranteed. Medicare Part B enrollment is in progress.

Can we use the Step Up For Students scholarship for speech therapy?

The practice is applying to become an approved provider for Florida's Step Up For Students Family Empowerment Scholarship for Students with Unique Abilities. Once approved, eligible families can use scholarship funds for evaluations and therapy. Approval is in progress.

Do you offer therapy packages?

Yes. A package of eight 60 minute sessions is $1,280, about 9 percent less than paying per session. Packages suit families who already know therapy will continue for a couple of months and want a simple, predictable cost.

Swallowing and VitalStim

What are the signs of a swallowing problem in older adults?

Common signs include coughing or throat clearing during or after meals, a wet or gurgly voice after drinking, food that feels stuck, meals that take much longer than they used to, unplanned weight loss, and repeated chest infections. If you notice these, ask the physician about a swallowing evaluation with a speech language pathologist.

What is VitalStim therapy?

VitalStim is a form of neuromuscular electrical stimulation used alongside swallowing exercises. Small electrodes on the neck deliver gentle stimulation while the patient practices swallowing. Whitney is VitalStim certified, and she decides with each patient's physician whether it is appropriate as part of a treatment plan.

Can a speech therapist help with swallowing after a stroke?

Yes. Swallowing difficulty, called dysphagia, is common after a stroke, and a speech language pathologist evaluates how safely a person swallows, recommends the right food and drink textures, and teaches exercises and strategies. Whitney's flagship adult specialty is swallowing.

What are IDDSI diet levels?

IDDSI is the International Dysphagia Diet Standardisation Initiative, a shared scale for how thick drinks should be and how soft or finely textured foods should be for people with swallowing difficulty. Using one scale helps families, kitchens, and care staff prepare meals the same way.

Brain injury and concussion

Can speech therapy help after a traumatic brain injury?

Yes. After a traumatic brain injury, a speech language pathologist works on attention, memory, organization, word finding, social communication, and swallowing, depending on what the injury affected. Traumatic brain injury is one of Whitney's two specialties, for adults and for school age children.

What is cognitive communication therapy?

Cognitive communication therapy targets the thinking skills that support communication, such as attention, memory, planning, and problem solving. It helps people after a brain injury, a stroke, or with a neurological condition manage daily conversations, appointments, and tasks more independently.

How does speech therapy help a child return to school after a concussion?

A speech language pathologist can assess attention, processing speed, memory, and language after a concussion, then work with the family and the school on a return to learn plan. That may include temporary classroom supports while the student recovers.

04

School age children

Does my child need speech therapy?

Consider an evaluation if your school age child is hard to understand, has trouble following directions or telling a story in order, struggles with reading and writing because of language, stutters, or finds conversations with peers difficult. An evaluation answers the question clearly and gives you a written plan either way.

What ages do you see?

The practice sees school age children, about ages 5 to 18, and adults of any age, with a focus on older adults. School age sessions cover speech sounds, expressive and receptive language, language for reading and writing, social communication, and stuttering.

Where do children's sessions happen?

School age children are seen at home after school, so families skip the drive. Parents are welcome in sessions and leave with clear activities to practice at home.

Do you work with my child's school or IEP team?

Yes, with your written permission. Whitney can review an IEP or 504 plan, share evaluation results with the school, and coordinate goals so private therapy and school support reinforce each other.

Home and facility visits

Do speech therapists come to your home in Orlando?

Yes. The Visiting Voice is a mobile practice. Older adults are seen at home, in assisted living, in memory care, or in skilled nursing, across Lake Nona and greater Orlando. Children are seen at home after school.

What areas do you serve?

Lake Nona, Orlando, Winter Park, Kissimmee, St. Cloud, Celebration, Dr. Phillips, Windermere, Oviedo, and Lake Mary. If you are nearby but outside these areas, ask. We will tell you honestly whether a visit is practical.

Can you see my parent in assisted living?

Yes. Visits happen in the resident's apartment or a quiet space in the community, and the therapist coordinates with nursing and wellness staff so everyone follows the same recommendations.

Do you partner with senior living communities and skilled nursing facilities?

Yes. The practice offers PRN and vacancy coverage, scheduled on site therapy days, and staff in services on topics like modified diets and dementia communication. Facility coverage is priced by agreement, and staff in services are $350 per session.

Getting started

Do I need a doctor's referral for speech therapy?

Not for private pay. For Medicare, a physician, nurse practitioner, or physician assistant must certify the plan of care, and the practice can send that request to the doctor for you. Either way, findings are shared with the physician when you agree.

What happens at a speech therapy evaluation?

At an evaluation, the therapist reviews history, observes and tests speech, language, thinking, or swallowing depending on the concern, and talks through what matters most to the patient and family. You receive a written report with results, recommendations, and a plan.

How long does speech therapy take?

It depends on the goals, not a fixed program. The plan of care states how often visits happen and for how long, and progress is reviewed with the family regularly. When goals are met, therapy steps down or ends with a home program.

How do I get started?

Request a consultation through the website. After a short conversation to understand the situation, you can complete the new patient intake online and schedule the evaluation.
