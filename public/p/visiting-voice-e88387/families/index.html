For families

Help for your parent or child, and for you.

Arranging care for a mother or father, often while working and sometimes from another city, is a lot to carry. So is watching a child struggle to be understood or to keep up in class. We see your parent at home or in their community, see your child at home after school, explain what we find in plain language, and keep you informed from the first call.

01Signs it may be time to call: older adults

Small changes at the table are often the first clue.

Families usually notice these before anyone else. If one or more sounds familiar, an evaluation can tell you what is happening and what will help. You do not need to be certain before you call.

In an emergency

Sudden trouble speaking, a drooping face, or weakness on one side can mean a stroke is happening now. Call 911 right away.

  • ✓Coughing, choking, or a wet, gurgly voice during meals
  • ✓Food pocketed in the cheeks or left in the mouth after eating
  • ✓Pneumonia or chest infections that keep coming back
  • ✓Trouble finding words, or mixing up words, after a stroke
  • ✓A voice that has become soft, hoarse, or hard to hear
  • ✓Slurred speech, or speech that is harder to understand than before
  • ✓Repeating questions, or losing the thread of conversations
  • ✓Meals taking much longer, weight loss, or avoiding favorite foods
02Signs it may be time to call: school age children

Parents and teachers often notice first.

If one or more of these sounds like your child, an evaluation can tell you what is going on and what will help. You do not need a referral or a diagnosis before you call.

School age care
  • ✓People outside the family have trouble understanding your child
  • ✓Sounds like r, s, l, or th still come out differently than expected for their age
  • ✓Trouble following directions or answering questions about what they heard
  • ✓Short or jumbled sentences, or difficulty explaining an idea
  • ✓Reading the words but not understanding them, or written work far behind spoken ideas
  • ✓Difficulty joining conversations, taking turns, or reading tone of voice
  • ✓Repeating sounds, getting stuck on words, or avoiding speaking in class
  • ✓A teacher or pediatrician has raised a concern about speech or language
03How it works

Three steps, and a person at every one.

You will talk with a licensed therapist who will help plan your parent's or child's care.

  1. 1

    A conversation first

    Call or send a request. We talk through what you are noticing, your loved one's history, and practical details like location, schedule, and whether Medicare or a scholarship applies.

  2. 2

    An evaluation where they are

    A licensed SLP completes a full assessment at home or in your parent's community, or at home for school age children, about 90 minutes including time with you. You receive a written report with findings and recommendations in plain language.

  3. 3

    Therapy with real updates

    Sessions on a set schedule, with the same therapist whenever possible. You get regular updates by phone or email, even if you live out of state, and progress reports go to the physician. Family members and parents are welcome to join sessions.

04Paying for care

What Medicare covers, and what it does not.

We review coverage and payment options with you before the first visit, so there are no surprises. The same rates apply to children and adults.

Medicare Part B

Covered with a doctor's order

Enrollment in progress. Medicare is not billed until the practice's enrollment is complete.

Part B covers outpatient speech therapy when it is medically necessary. Medicare requires a plan of care certified by your parent's doctor, nurse practitioner, or physician assistant. We prepare the plan and send it to them to certify.

After the yearly Part B deductible, Medicare generally pays 80% of the approved amount. A supplemental plan may cover the rest.

Medicare Advantage: the practice is not in network with these plans at this time.

Private pay

Simple, published rates

Families choose private pay for school age therapy, for services Medicare does not cover, for extra caregiver coaching, or when there is no Medicare coverage. We can also give you a superbill to submit to your own insurance.

Good to know

What is not covered

  • Visits without a certified plan of care, when billing Medicare
  • Our visits during a Medicare covered skilled nursing stay or open home health episode, since the facility or agency provides therapy then
  • Sessions that do not serve a treatment goal in the plan of care

If a service may not be covered, we tell you in writing before the visit. Once enrollment is complete, Medicare Part B covered services for Medicare beneficiaries are billed to Medicare with a certified plan of care.

For school age children

Families can pay privately, ask for a superbill to submit to their own insurance, or, once the practice's provider approval is complete, use Florida's Step Up For Students Unique Abilities scholarship where eligible. Most school age sessions are 45 minutes, at $135. Each home session adds a flat $25 travel fee.

This is general information to help you plan, not billing or insurance advice. Coverage depends on the plan and medical needs.

05Questions families ask

Straight answers before you call.

See all questions and answers

Do we need a doctor's order?

For Medicare, yes. Medicare requires a plan of care certified by your parent's doctor, nurse practitioner, or physician assistant. We can send the request to your parent's doctor for you. For private pay, an order is not required, though we still share our findings with the physician when you agree.

Does Medicare cover speech therapy at home?

Medicare Part B covers outpatient speech therapy when it is medically necessary, including visits at home or in assisted living. Your parent does not need to be homebound. The usual Part B deductible and coinsurance apply. The practice's Medicare enrollment is in progress, so Medicare is not billed yet.

How long will therapy take?

It depends on the goals, not a fixed program. The plan of care states how often we will visit and for how long, and we review progress with you at least every ten visits. When goals are met, we step down or finish with a home program.

Can I be there, or join from out of town?

Yes, and we encourage it. Family members can sit in on sessions, and we can include you by phone or video. After each visit, you can expect a short update on what we worked on and what to practice.

Mom lives in assisted living. Can you see her there?

Yes. We see residents in their apartment or a quiet space in the community and coordinate with the nursing and wellness staff so everyone follows the same recommendations.

What if Dad is already getting home health?

While a home health agency has an open episode of care, therapy is usually provided through that agency. We can begin when the episode ends, and we are glad to talk with the agency's team about a smooth handoff.

What if my parent does not want therapy?

That is common, and it is worth a conversation. We start with what matters to them, such as enjoying a favorite meal safely or talking with grandchildren on the phone, and build therapy around it.

Do you see children?

Yes. School age children, about ages 5 to 18, are one of the practice's two focus areas. Whitney works with kids on speech sounds, expressive and receptive language, language for reading and writing, social communication, and stuttering.

Can we use the Step Up scholarship?

We are applying to become an approved provider for the Step Up For Students Unique Abilities scholarship; approval is in progress. Once approved, eligible families can use their scholarship for evaluations and therapy. Families can also pay privately or ask for a superbill to submit to their own insurance.

Where do sessions happen?

Older adults are seen at home, in assisted living, in memory care, or in skilled nursing. School age children are seen at home after school.

What areas do you serve?

Lake Nona, Orlando, Winter Park, Kissimmee, St. Cloud, Celebration, Dr. Phillips, Windermere, Oviedo, and Lake Mary. If you are nearby but outside these areas, ask us.
