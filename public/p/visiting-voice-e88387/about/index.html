About the practice

Care for the people who raised us.

Whitney Albert, M.S., CCC-SLP

Whitney Albert has practiced speech language pathology since 2012, giving the same patience to a child learning to be understood as to an elder relearning how to swallow safely. A graduate of Florida State University, she holds the ASHA Certificate of Clinical Competence and is licensed in Florida. Her specialties are swallowing, where she is VitalStim certified, and traumatic brain injury. She founded The Visiting Voice so that the people who raised us, and the children growing up now, receive care that is unhurried, personal, and delivered with dignity.

01Her story

Why this practice exists.

The Visiting Voice rests on a single belief: therapy means the most where life actually happens. At the kitchen table. In a favorite armchair. In the room where homework gets done.

For older adults, it means one clinician who comes to know your loved one, arrives on a predictable schedule, and translates every goal into plain language for the family and the physician. For school age children, it means the same constancy: one therapist, goals written with care, and parents who leave each session knowing exactly what to practice at home.

On the name. Care is most meaningful when it meets you where you are. And so, the practice comes to you.

CLINICAL FOCUS
School age children and older adults. For school age children: speech sounds, expressive and receptive language, language for reading and writing, social communication, fluency, return to learn after a concussion or brain injury.
02What we believe

Four promises behind every visit.

These are the standards the practice is built on, for every patient, every family, and every community we serve.

01

Care comes to the patient

Therapy happens at home, in assisted living, or at the bedside, where your loved one is most comfortable and where everyday skills actually get used.

02

Families are part of the plan

Caregivers are invited into sessions, trained on safe swallowing and communication strategies, and asked what matters most to them.

03

The same therapist whenever possible

Your loved one works with one named clinician who knows their history, their preferences, and exactly where they are in their recovery.

04

Clear goals, plain language updates

Every plan has written, measurable goals, and progress is shared with family and physicians in words anyone can follow.
