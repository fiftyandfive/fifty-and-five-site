Motor Speech

Dysarthria and apraxia of speech affect the muscles and the planning behind speech after stroke, brain injury, ALS, multiple sclerosis, and other neurological conditions. Your parent knows exactly what to say, but the words come out slurred, slow, or effortful.

SIGNS IT MAY HELP

  • Slurred or mumbled speech that others struggle to understand
  • Groping for the right mouth position, or errors that change each time
  • Running out of breath while talking
  • Speech that gets harder as the day goes on

WHAT A SESSION LOOKS LIKE

Exercises for breath support, articulation, and speaking rate, practiced with the words and phrases your parent uses most. When speech is severely affected, we introduce other ways to communicate, from simple letter boards to speech generating devices.

All questions and answers

What happens at a speech therapy evaluation?

At an evaluation, the therapist reviews history, observes and tests speech, language, thinking, or swallowing depending on the concern, and talks through what matters most to the patient and family. You receive a written report with results, recommendations, and a plan.

How long does speech therapy take?

It depends on the goals, not a fixed program. The plan of care states how often visits happen and for how long, and progress is reviewed with the family regularly. When goals are met, therapy steps down or ends with a home program.
